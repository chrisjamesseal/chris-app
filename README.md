# My Trips

One place for your group trip, flights, money, plans and photos. One organiser starts the trip, everyone else gets a link, and now everyone can pitch in.

**Live app:** https://chrisjamesseal.github.io/cals-trippin/

## How it works

- **No accounts.** You start a trip and share one link. Anyone with the link can open it on any device, no sign-up.
- **Collaborative editing (Milestone 2).** With the shared store switched on (see below), the link is **permanent** and everyone with it can edit: flights, notes, documents, receipts and the mood board, and see each other's changes. Each person picks their name once, so the **edit log** can show who changed what and when (the **Log** tab).
- **Receipt splitting.** Add a receipt as an even split or item-by-item, tick who had each item, and the app works out what's left to assign and what each person owes. No manual maths.
- **Travel:** Flights, Ferries and Car Hire in one place. Flights offer airline and airport autocomplete (code shown large, airport name in grey), tick-boxes for what's included with the ticket (cabin/checked bag, priority…), a link to live flight status, and you can attach the boarding pass or any document to each item. **No flights booked yet shows the same dashed card** as an empty accommodation night, with its own *Add Flight* button, instead of a plain message.
- **Accommodation:** add as many stays as the trip needs (hotel, Airbnb, a friend's sofa…), each with an address (a one-tap Google Maps link, which carries Google's own icon, and a 🔗 button that copies it - the address isn't printed on the card, since those two buttons are what you'd do with it), check-in/check-out date and time, a booking reference and a link to the listing. Stays are built from **the same card as a flight** - a title bar carrying the name, with the date, or on the Itinerary the time you can get in ("From 15:00") or have to be out ("Until 11:00"), to its right - so the two read as the same kind of thing wherever they sit next to each other. **Nights with nowhere booked show up as a dashed card** in the list, sitting exactly where the gap falls, saying how many nights and which ones; its *Add Accommodation* button opens the form with those dates already filled in. A stay covers the nights from its check-in up to the night before its check-out, and the trip needs beds from its start date to the night before its end date, since that's the day you travel home.
- **Itinerary:** the full day-by-day schedule: your flights and accommodation check-in/check-out appear here automatically alongside the stops you add by hand (a place, a time or just a morning/afternoon/evening slot, and a note on why it's worth it). Once a trip has anything on it, **every day of it shows**, not just the ones with something planned; a day with nothing yet gets the same dashed card as an accommodation gap, sitting exactly where it falls, with its own *Add Stop* button pre-filled to that day. Adding a stop or an idea from a particular day carries that day into the form, so you never pick it twice - from the day's own dashed card, or from the crumb buttons while a single day is showing. A stop's **When** can be *All day* (which sits at the head of its day, and isn't spelled out on the card itself since it's the default), a part of the day, or a specific time. A travel leg you haven't picked a mode for yet reads *Travel to Kassiopi not decided*. Flights, ferries, car hire and travel legs can all carry an optional **Link** field (a booking confirmation, timetable or check-in page), shown as a small icon on the card once set. Flights and stays carry the airline's or booking site's logo as a small square on the card, dropping the redundant text next to it to save space when a logo loads (the flight number stays); airline entry is a searchable picker rather than a plain text box. A stretch of nights with nowhere booked shows up here too, not just on the Accommodation tab, right where the gap falls. Nothing on the page deletes itself: tapping a card always opens its edit form first, where *Delete* lives alongside *Save*, same as every other section. Booking/map buttons on itinerary cards are icon-only (a favicon, a pin), no label. Give a stop a location to pin it on an embedded Google Map, and add a photo. Ideas pins carry their image, location and emoji straight in. Everything on the day-by-day view is labelled with an emoji (your stops, travel legs, flights, ferries, car hire, check in/out). Travel legs between places carry a **Leave by / Arrive by** toggle, so a time on a journey says which end of it it refers to rather than leaving you to guess. Every flight also gets a suggested trip to the airport, set to **arrive two hours before departure** (rounded down to the quarter hour, and never rolled back past midnight so it stays on the flight's own day); tap it to fill in how you're getting there, and change the time like any other leg. **Moving between two places to stay** gets the same treatment: check out of one and into another on the same day and the journey between them is suggested, sitting on the itinerary between the check-out and the check-in, pinned to the check-out time since that's when you have to be out. Both suggestions are real Add Travel cards, just not stored until you save one, at which point the suggestion stops appearing beside it.
- **Tasks:** a place for anything you know you'll need but haven't sorted yet, a hotel by the airport for the night before an early flight, say, that doesn't fit the Accommodation or Travel gaps because it isn't one, it's just something you haven't got to. Each one is a name, an optional day and **When** (all day, a part of the day, or a specific time, the same picker a stop uses), and optional notes. Give it a day and it appears on the Itinerary on that day, at that time, alongside everything else. Leave the day off and it sits under **Anytime** at the foot of the Itinerary **in red**, on both screens, as the thing still needing a decision, which is the point: as the plan fills in, what's still undefined is what's coloured. Tick it when it's done (done ones drop under a **Completed** heading) or delete it. There's no edit button, the card itself opens the form, the same way nothing on the Itinerary deletes itself. A travel leg can go without a mode chosen yet - its own **Not Decided** option (a different question from whether it's booked, which for every travel item now just means it's on the trip or it isn't) - and shows as a plain text line on the Itinerary instead of a card, the same as any other travel leg that isn't a flight or ferry. A stretch of nights with nowhere booked shows up here too, worked out fresh each time rather than something you could accidentally delete. A card that's otherwise filled in but still missing one key detail (an airline, an operator, a company) shows just that in red with a ❓ instead of leaving it blank. The Tasks dashboard tile counts all of this too, not just what you've typed in by hand - a stretch of nights with nowhere booked and a day with nothing planned both count as one thing still to sort out, alongside your own hand-typed tasks.
- **Weather on the itinerary:** each day shows its forecast (an emoji for the conditions, the high/low and the chance of rain) from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/), which is free and needs no API key or sign-up. **Nothing to set up:** the app works out where you are on each day by walking the trip in order, a flight, ferry or accommodation check-in moves you on, and the days in between inherit wherever you were last, so a multi-country trip shows the right town's weather on the right days. Where a hotel name means nothing to a map, it falls back to the airport's city and then to the country. Forecasts only run about two weeks ahead, so days beyond that show nothing rather than a guess, and if the forecast can't be reached the itinerary is unaffected, it just doesn't show weather.
- **Documents:** the app is a hub: upload booking confirmations, tickets and insurance PDFs/photos so everyone can open them from one place, and link out to things that live in other apps (airline app, check-in page).
- **Structured notes:** emergency contacts and general trip info (visas, house rules…).
- **Packing list:** a shared list for the trip, with a one-tap starter list of the usual essentials. **Ticking is per person**, so everyone gets their own boxes against the same list and an item only counts as fully packed once everyone who needs it has ticked it; assign an item to one person when it's only theirs to bring. A progress bar tracks the whole list, and as departure gets close the section reminds you how much you've still got to do. **Remind me**, next to the section title, saves a calendar reminder (a standard `.ics` file, so it works on iPhone, Android and desktop) for the evening before you travel, listing everything still unpacked.
- **Money splitter:** receipts with itemised line-by-line splitting or an even-split toggle, plus a who-pays-whom settlement that combines debts into the fewest possible payments (Tricount-style). Your own balance shows right on the dashboard's Money tile.
- **Photos:** links out to one shared album everyone adds to. **PhotoCircle** is recommended (free, cross-platform, and everyone, iPhone, Android or web, can *add* photos, not just view); Apple Shared Album / Google Photos links also work if you already use them, with in-app notes on each one's limitations.
- **Mood board:** pin links, notes and images; built-in destination-based idea suggestions. Each idea carries an **emoji**, suggested from its title as you type (sushi gets a sushi, a hike gets boots, a rooftop bar gets a cocktail) and overridable with a quick pick or any emoji you type yourself.
- **Polls:** settle group decisions ("where are we eating Friday?", "beach or city day?") without scrolling back through a chat. Ask a question, add the options, everyone taps their pick. Each option shows the running count, a share-of-the-vote bar and who voted for it, so you can see where things stand and who you're still waiting on. One vote each by default (keyed to the name you picked for the trip); tap your choice again to take it back or tap another to change your mind. A poll can also **allow selecting multiple answers**, set when it's created, in which case each tap toggles that option on or off independently. Anyone answering an open poll can add another option on the spot too, not just whoever created it. Close a poll once it's decided and the winner is highlighted, or reopen it if plans change.
- **Places I've Been:** the app's **home page** (a bare `#` hash) is a hub of four short, row-shaped cards - an emoji on the left, the count and its label together on one line - each a way into the world: **Places Been** 📍 (everywhere you've gone), **Going** ✈️ (trips you've got planned), **Want to Go** ❤️ (your wish list) and **Countries** 🌍 (every country in the world). Below the cards sits **Your Trips**, so the whole front door is one page; a **+ New Trip** button sits next to the My Travels title. A country on a trip counts as **Been** once that trip has finished and **Going** until then, so somewhere you're flying to next month isn't claimed as somewhere you've already been; a country you've already been to that you're also going back to counts in both. Ticking Been yourself always wins over a trip, and unticking it (even one a trip put there) sticks, so a return visit doesn't quietly undo your correction.

  Tapping **Places I've Been** opens a two-column list: a `#` for visit order top-left, a flag, the name, and a delete icon top-right to take it back off, most recently visited at the top, numbered from the bottom up so #1 is the first place you went. Two tabs switch how it's read: **🔢 In Order** is that one numbered run, **🌍 Continents** groups the same cards under Europe, Asia and the rest with a count on each. The numbers are worked out once over the whole list, so a country keeps the same number in either view - the continent tab regroups the cards, it doesn't renumber them. A country on a booked trip that hasn't happened yet sits in that same run rather than a section of its own: at the top, carrying its own number, faded and with nothing to tap, until the trip's dates pass and it becomes an ordinary entry. Tapping **Want to Go** or **Countries** opens a row of region tiles (Europe, Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Oceania), each just an icon and a name, no count underneath; the full country list, grouped the same way, sits right below them rather than behind a tap on an "All" tile - tapping one region tile instead narrows the list to just that one, with a way back to every region. Countries' region tiles show the full country list for that region either way, not just the ones you've already categorised; each row carries a **Want to Go / Not Exploring switch** instead of a tick, so marking a country off your list is a single tap either way - unless it's already on a booked trip, in which case there's nothing left to toggle and the switch is replaced by a fixed, greyed **Going** pill. Tapping **Going** scrolls straight down to **Your Trips**, the same list already sitting below the cards, since that's already the list of what's booked - no second copy of it on its own page.

  On every list screen, the **search box sits flush against the bottom bar**, within a thumb's reach and still there however far down the list you've scrolled. Each country shows its capital, language and a "Search Flights" link out to Google Flights. Includes England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland as their own entries, not lumped into a single United Kingdom.
- **Games** 🎮: a bottom-bar tab showing your PlayStation trophy progress on what you're playing (percentage complete and trophy count per game, most recently played first). PSN has no public API, so this needs a small proxy you deploy yourself, see [`psn-proxy/README.md`](psn-proxy/README.md); the tab shows setup instructions until `PSN_PROXY_URL` is configured, and after that a one-time "paste your NPSSO code" step to connect the account. The account connection itself lives only in your browser's `localStorage`, on that one device.
- **Plants** 🥕: a bottom-bar tab for tracking whatever vegetables you're growing. Each one moves through six stages (Seed Sown → Sprouted → Growing → Flowering/Fruiting → Ready to Harvest → Harvested), shown as a dot row on its card; tapping a card opens the same form to edit the stage, planted date or watering settings. A **Water Every (Days)** setting per vegetable, together with a **Watered Today** button that stamps today's date, is what drives the one tip shown on each card - overdue watering always takes priority over everything else, then how close it is to harvest, otherwise a plain "Looking good". Cards are emoji-first and centre-aligned: typing a name that matches a common vegetable (tomato, carrot, pepper…) picks its emoji automatically, or pick any of them by hand from the grid in the form. Home shows whichever vegetable needs attention most.
- **The app's look:** teal/coral for a while, then a brief run with a switchable App Style picker; now settled on **Sunset Postcard** as the one look - terracotta and plum, warm serif headings (Fraunces), soft rounded corners. It's the whole palette (colours, corner radius, shadow, typeface) as a handful of CSS custom properties in `:root`, so a future look would again just be a new value set, not a rewrite.
- **Trips without dates yet:** plenty of trips start as "somewhere hot next summer" long before anything is booked, so a trip can carry a **rough idea of when** instead of two exact dates: a month, a season, a range of months, or just a year, plus roughly how long you'll be away (a day, a weekend, a long weekend, or a number of weeks). A trip pinned down that loosely runs on **day numbers**: its itinerary reads *Day 1, Day 2, Day 3* rather than inventing calendar dates, and the stop and travel forms offer a Day picker in place of the date field. Set real dates later and each day number becomes the date it lands on (Day 3 of a trip starting the 10th becomes the 12th); switch back to a rough idea and the dates fold into day numbers again, so the plan survives the move either way. Anything that needs real dates (weather, the countdown, auto-archiving, accommodation gaps) simply waits until there are some, and the trip card says **Being Planned** rather than counting down to nothing.
- **Dates** are written out in full throughout ("24 September 2026"), and a range says the year once at the end ("24 September - 26 September 2026") unless it runs into the new year, where both halves need their own ("28 December 2026 - 3 January 2027").
- **Auto-archiving:** trips grey out and drop to the bottom of the list two weeks after the end date - still fully editable, archiving is a visual/sort state, not a lock. **Your Trips** runs as one chronological line (soonest first, so a trip that just ended stays right at the top of what's left) for those two weeks, then the archived trips follow at the end, most recently ended first.
- **Your trips are kept:** trips are saved in the browser, survive app updates, and with the shared store configured sync to everyone holding the link, so they are already backed up off-device. (Export/import still exists in the code as `App.exportTrips()` / `App.importTrips()` from the browser console, but there are no buttons for it: the cloud copy makes manual backups redundant for normal use.)

## Turning on the shared, permanent link (Firebase)

The app works out of the box **on a single device** with no setup. To get everyone-sees-every-trip syncing plus the shared PIN gate, connect a free [Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/) Realtime Database:

1. Create a free Firebase project and add a **Realtime Database**.
2. In Project settings → *Your apps* → *Web*, copy the config object.
3. Paste it into `FIREBASE_CONFIG` near the top of the `<script>` in `index.html` (these are public client keys, safe to commit).
4. Set the database rules. Access is gated by a shared PIN (no per-user logins), so for a friends-only trip planner:

   ```json
   {
     "rules": {
       "trips": {
         ".read": true,
         "$id": { ".write": true }
       },
       "places": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "gigs": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "films": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "filmsWatchlist": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "filmLeaderboardOrder": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "theatre": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "theatreWatchlist": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "theatreLeaderboardOrder": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "plants": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "gameRatings": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "gameLeaderboardOrder": { ".read": true, ".write": true },
       "gameWishlist": { ".read": true, ".write": true }
     }
   }
   ```

   Note the `trips` node needs its own top-level `.read: true`, Realtime Database rules don't cascade upward from `trips/$id` to `trips`, and the app needs to read the *whole* `trips` node at once to list every trip on the "Your Trips" page. Without it, the app silently falls back to only showing trips this particular browser already knows about (looks like "no trips saved"). The same non-cascading rule applies to `places` (the shared Places I've Been checklist), `gigs` (the Gigs log), `films` (the Films log), `filmsWatchlist` (the Films tab's Watchlist), `filmLeaderboardOrder` (the Films tab's Leaderboard), `theatreLeaderboardOrder` (the Theatre tab's Leaderboard), `plants` (the Plants tracker), `gameRatings` (every game you've rated), `gameLeaderboardOrder` (the Games tab's Leaderboard) and `gameWishlist` (the Games tab's Play List/Wishlist): without their own rule, anything ticked, logged, rated or reordered stays stuck on the device that did it instead of syncing to the group, and is only ever as safe as that one browser's storage - clear site data, switch devices, or reconnect after that storage is gone, and it's unrecoverable. The app shows a "Not Saved" error naming the missing rule the next time it tries to write to a path that isn't covered, so it's worth adding all of these up front rather than only once something's noticed missing.

That's it, the app now shows every trip that exists (not just ones this browser made), and share buttons hand out `…/#/trip/<id>` links that stay the same forever and sync everyone's edits. Without a config, the app quietly falls back to single-device mode, a fixed local PIN, and the Share button produces a portable snapshot link instead.

## Notes

- Static site, no backend of its own, hosted on GitHub Pages. The optional shared store is Firebase, called directly from the browser. The optional Games tab is the one exception: PlayStation's API can't be called from a browser at all, so it needs a small proxy you deploy yourself (see `psn-proxy/README.md`).
- **Security is deliberately light:** the PIN gate is a client-side deterrent, not real access control, anyone who reads the page source can find it. It stops casual visitors, not a determined stranger. Keep the URL and PIN to your group.
- **Changing the PIN needs a deploy.** It lives in `APP_PIN` near the top of the `<script>` in `index.html`, and nowhere else. It used to be editable in-app (stored in Firebase), but that meant anyone who got past the gate once could lock everyone else out, so the in-app screen was removed. If you set a PIN through that old screen, delete the leftover `appPin` node in your Firebase console: it is no longer read, so it does nothing, it is just stale.
- Large uploaded files and mood-board images stay on the uploader's device (only smaller ones sync); their details still appear for everyone.
- **Every external link** (flight search, maps, booking sites, album links, video links, design articles) hands off to Safari itself rather than Apple's cut-down in-app browser, when the app is installed to an iPhone home screen. Home-screen web apps otherwise open links in a stripped-down view with no saved logins, no password autofill and no app handoff; `App.openExternal` in `index.html` sends the same URL to Safari with an undocumented `x-safari-https:` scheme first, falling back to a normal new tab if that does nothing (every other platform is unaffected). PDFs you've attached are the one exception - those are local files, not external links, and open in the device's own PDF viewer directly.

There is **no top header**: the app opens straight onto its content, and everything the header used to hold now lives on the page or the bottom bar (the version number and *What's new* sit at the foot of Your Trips). Every page starts its title at the same distance down the screen, so nothing shifts as you move between them. On a trip's overview, **Share Trip** sits above **Delete Trip**, so the destructive button is the last thing on the page.

The **bottom bar** is the main navigation everywhere: Travel (the app's front door, labelled Travel but still the Places hub underneath - Your Trips lives right there below the four cards, so Trips no longer has its own tab), Films, Music, Games, Plants, a green **+** in the middle, and the ☰ Menu (the ☰ Menu is inert outside a trip; the others work everywhere). Itinerary isn't on the bar - it's still on the Overview dashboard and in the trip menu like every other section, it just isn't one of the five things always in thumb's reach. Inside a trip the **+ fans out into a wheel** of the things you add most: an idea, a stop, a receipt, travel and a document. They spring out of the circle and fold back into it rather than appearing and vanishing on the spot. They're icon-only (each carries its name as a tooltip and to screen readers), and the + itself becomes the close button; tapping away or pressing escape also closes it. Everything else is added from its own section, which is where you'd be anyway. Outside a trip, or on an archived one, there's nothing to add but a new trip, so the + does that directly. **Your Trips** also has a *+ New Trip* button at the top right.

There are **no pop-up notifications**. Actions don't announce themselves (you just did them), deletes ask before they happen rather than offering an undo afterwards, and the handful of messages you genuinely need to read (a save that failed, something that has to be filled in first) appear in a box that waits until you dismiss it. Inside a trip, everything is also reached from the **dashboard tiles** (each with its own emoji): Overview, Itinerary, Tasks, Travel (flights, ferries, car hire), Accommodation, Ideas, Packing, Polls, Money, Photos, Documents & Links, Notes, Emergency Contacts and Activity: no horizontal tab scrolling. Countries and people are managed from the **Edit Trip** button on the Overview, sitting above Share Trip.

Built from the v1 product spec (`cals_trippin_spec.md`); this release adds **Milestone 2 (collaborative editing + edit log)** and a branded UI refresh (teal/coral palette, "My Trips" wordmark, icon menu navigation).
